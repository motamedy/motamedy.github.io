Gliese 445

You found a star your eyes can't see.

Gliese 445 is a small red dwarf about 17 light-years away in Camelopardalis, not far from the North Star. At magnitude 10.8 it is about 50 times too faint for the naked eye, even under a perfect sky.

How far your eyes reach depends on how bright a star really is, not only how close it is:

  • In a truly dark sky the eye reaches about magnitude 6.5. A star like our Sun would disappear beyond about 70 light-years.
  • Over Mashhad on a clear night the limit is about magnitude 4, so a Sun-like star would disappear beyond about 20 light-years.
  • Gliese 445 would have to be within about 2.4 light-years to be seen: closer than any star we know.
  • The most luminous stars carry much farther. Deneb is roughly 2,600 light-years away, and the farthest thing your eyes can see is the Andromeda Galaxy, 2.5 million light-years away.

Voyager 1 is heading this way. In about 40,000 years it will pass within 1.6 light-years of Gliese 445, and from there the star would finally be bright enough to see, at about magnitude 5.6.
